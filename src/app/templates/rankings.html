What is the trend score?

The trend score (0–100) measures how strongly a stock's price has been rising compared with every other stock. It's calculated from the stock's own price history only. There's no model or forecast behind it.

How it's calculated

  1. Slopes. A straight line is fit to the log of the daily price over the last 5 years, 1 year and 1 month. Each slope is the % change along that line.
  2. Percentiles. Each slope is ranked against all other stocks, so a 1-year percentile of 80 means the stock's 1-year slope beats 80% of stocks.
  3. Blend. The trend score is the weighted average of the three percentiles: {% if meta %}5-year {{ (meta.weights['5y'] * 100) | round | int }}%, 1-year {{ (meta.weights['1y'] * 100) | round | int }}%, 1-month {{ (meta.weights['1m'] * 100) | round | int }}%{% endif %}. You can change these with Tune weights.

What it isn't

The trend score describes past momentum. It is not a prediction of where the price goes next. A score of 90 means the stock has been trending better than about 90% of stocks, not that it will keep doing so.

How it relates to the prediction model

The upcoming Portfolio Sandbox will use a tree-based model to forecast each stock's price 30 days out. That model uses the same slope calculations as inputs, over more windows (1 year, 6 months, 3 months, 1 month, 2 weeks and 3 days), but it's a separate system. Once it's built, its forecast can appear next to the trend score.
